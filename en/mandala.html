✦ What is a Mandala?

A Mandala is a circle — a fundamental structure of the universe. Mandala motifs appear in prehistoric drawings on different continents. The Sanskrit word “Mandala” means the whole world, a sacred circle, a centre or the circle of life. In Tibetan Buddhism it is also a point: a sacred space symbolising the original source of all creation.

A Mandala may be a diagram drawn on the ground, fabric or wood. Sometimes it is made from coloured sand or rice or placed on temple walls. Precious stones have also been used to create mandalas.

The simplest mandala is circular, but it can take other forms: a star, floral motif, square, circle within a square, polygon, ellipse, ring, shell, planet or circles of any kind.

✦ Historical traces of Mandalas

Mandalas have appeared in cultures throughout the world. Well-known stone circles include Stonehenge in England, described as a sacred place of Celtic druids, and Odry, Węsiory and Ślęża in Poland. Circle symbolism was familiar to Celts, Germanic peoples, Native Americans and Slavs, among others.

Traces of mandalas can be found in rock drawings in the Americas, Africa and Europe. Circular plans were used for temples and tombs in China. Mandalas also appeared in Aztec culture, including its calendar, and on painted ceramics from Romania, Ukraine and the Balkans.

Mandala-like arrangements of floors and images occur in churches, cathedrals, temples and other places of worship. In Tibet, the monks' ritual of creating mandalas remains alive today.

✦ The meaning of a Mandala

Creating and contemplating a Mandala is a form of meditation. Mandalas focus on energy centres (chakras), which they can cleanse, harmonise and strengthen. They are created in stillness, reaching towards deeper sources of expression.

Creating a Mandala through free expression enables inner transformation and has a powerful therapeutic effect. Swiss psychiatrist Carl Gustav Jung first used Mandalas in psychotherapy. In his theory, the Mandala represents the entirety of the psyche, both conscious and unconscious.

✦ The process of creating a Mandala

Before creating a Mandala, I choose a suitable place: a secluded space without distractions. I quiet my thoughts and, in relaxation, create room for creativity.

Before I begin, I enter a relaxed, peaceful state of meditation. I clear my thoughts and harmonise my subtle bodies, sometimes adding chanting and energetic cleansing.

During meditation, I connect spiritually, through the Higher Self, with the person for whom I am creating the Mandala. Once balanced, I begin: choosing materials, intuitively selecting colours and allowing the process to unfold. I note down insights that arise while painting.

When I reach complete stillness, the Personal Mandala is finished.
